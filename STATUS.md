Source of the question: Balazi, Allaire, Jolivet and Omnes, *Inf-sup stable non-conforming finite elements on tetrahedra with second- and third-order accuracy*, Numerical Methods for Partial Differential Equations 41(6) (2025), e70057, doi:10.1002/num.70057; open version hal-04541809v3 (Appendix A; Remarks 3.2 and 3.6).

* **Proved in writing:** Theorem 2.1 (injective on P_(n+1); 7-dimensional kernel on P_(n+2) with explicit ridge basis; deficit (n-1)(n-2)/2; surjective on P_(n+3); minimum degree n+2 for n=1,2 and n+3 for n>=3); Proposition 3.2 (A vanishes on P_5 and A(Delta_4) = 1/34650); Corollary 5.2 (minimal enrichment count); Theorem 5.4 (vertex-invariant lean elements exist for every n); Corollary 5.8 (50-dimensional fourth-order element); Proposition 5.9 (14-monomial basis); Theorem 6.1, Proposition 6.2 and Corollary 6.3 (d-simplex kernel, deficit closed form, triangles); the Fortin and error estimates of Section 7 (established framework).
* **Exact computation:** rank tables for n=1..6, ridges, A(Delta_4), Gram matrix, 14-monomial determinant and invariance (`code/verify_tetrahedron.py`); d-simplex deficits and ranks for d=2,4,5,6 (`code/verify_dimensions.py`, full rank modulo the prime 2^61-1 certifies rank over Q); lean elements Delta_4 P_(n-3) for n=3..8, explicit n=4,5 elements and the S4 character identity sgn x Sym^(n-3)(std) (`code/verify_lean_element.py`); an independently coded reconstruction (`code/independent_check.py`).
* **Numerical:** the Stokes test of Section 8 (rates consistent with fourth-order convergence; computed discrete inf-sup values 0.40, 0.38, 0.37, 0.37 for N=1..4) and conditioning figures of Section 9. Mesh independence of the inf-sup constant comes from the Fortin argument, not from the numbers.
* **Conjecture:** Conjecture 5.6 (Delta_4 P_(n-3) is a lean enrichment for every n); Conjecture 6.4 (degree n+3 suffices on every d-simplex, d>=4).

Scope: degree optimality is relative to these moments, single-cell polynomial spaces and reproduction of P_(n+1). No claim about split-cell, nonpolynomial or virtual element spaces, pointwise divergence-free or pressure-robust schemes, p-uniform stability, curved elements, discrete Korn inequalities or solver speed.
